    Inference¶

    Marginal likelihood¶

    For fixed \(\theta\) the model is \(m = M\beta\) with the design matrix \(M = [R, D, \dots, T_0, \dots, T_K]\). With the prior \(\beta \sim \mathcal N(\mu, \Lambda)\) and the noise covariance \(C\),

    \[ \mathbf y \mid \theta \sim \mathcal N(M\mu,\; C + M\Lambda M^\top). \]

    With the whitening \(L^{-1}\) (\(C = LL^\top\)) and the Woodbury identity only a \(p\times p\) system is solved, \(p\) = number of amplitudes + K + 1:

    \[ A = \Lambda^{-1} + \tilde M^\top\tilde M,\quad b = \tilde M^\top \tilde r,\quad \ln L = -\tfrac12\left[\tilde r^\top\tilde r - b^\top A^{-1} b + \ln|C| + \ln|\Lambda| + \ln|A| + n\ln 2\pi\right] \]

    and \(\beta\mid\theta, y \sim \mathcal N(\mu + A^{-1}b, A^{-1})\). The tests compare this with the dense formula and with a numerical integral over \(\beta\).

    Linear and nonlinear parameters¶

    Each profile component has five nonlinear parameters, \(\theta = (v_\mathrm{ctr}, \Delta v, \Delta v_\mathrm{rand}, \alpha, f)\), and one linear amplitude, the integrated flux \(S\). The kernel still returns the ring and disk basis functions separately; the likelihood combines them into the unit-flux column \((1-f)R + fD\). An earlier version kept \(a_\mathrm{ring} = S(1-f)\) and \(a_\mathrm{disk} = Sf\) as two linear amplitudes. That is cheaper by one sampling dimension but has two drawbacks: for narrow profiles ring and disk are nearly identical and the two amplitudes become degenerate with large anti-correlated values, and the implied prior on \(f\) (the ratio of two half-normal amplitudes) depends on \(S\), flat for faint and peaked for bright sources. With \(f\) sampled, its prior is explicit and the same for every source, and the degeneracy becomes a flat direction in \(f\) that the prior handles.

    Amplitude positivity¶

    The amplitude \(S\) of each component has a half-normal prior (scale priors.amplitude_scale), i.e. a zero-mean Gaussian truncated to \(S \ge 0\). The truncation enters the likelihood exactly through the orthant probability of the conditional Gaussian,

    \[ p(y \mid \theta, K) = p_\mathrm{gauss}(y \mid \theta, K)\; P(S \ge 0 \mid \theta, y, K)\, 2^{q}, \]

    with the normal CDF for one component and the bivariate normal CDF (Owen's T function) for two. For three or more components the product of the marginal probabilities is used in the sampling target and the remaining correction is estimated by Monte Carlo and applied as importance weight (hiprofile.inference.amplitudes). The fraction of Gaussian draws with a negative amplitude is reported as negative_fraction; with the half-normal prior in the target it is informational.

    Noise¶

    Per-channel \(\sigma\) comes from the input or a robust estimate (MAD of first differences, corrected for the channel correlation; optionally restricted to noise.signal_free ranges). The covariance between channels i and j is \(C_{ij} = \sigma_i\sigma_j\,\rho(|i-j|)\); masked channels are dropped, and the banded covariance is factorised once (banded Cholesky for NumPy, dense inverse factor for JAX). priors.noise_scale optionally fits a global scale factor.

    Real spectra are smoothed in the channel domain (spectrometer window, Hanning, regridding to a velocity grid, averaging of Doppler-shifted scans), which correlates neighbouring channels. noise.correlation:

    value correlation
    auto (default) the smoothing in model.response (hanning, kernel(...)) plus an estimated white part; white without smoothing
    estimate measured on the residuals of the robust pre-fit: white noise smoothed by an unknown symmetric kernel \([h_2, h_1, 1, h_1, h_2]\) with \(h \ge 0\) plus a white part, fitted to the variance ratios of the 2nd–4th to the 1st differences of the residuals; white, 3-tap and 5-tap models are chosen by AIC
    white, hanning, hanning-decimated, [ρ₁, ρ₂, …] fixed; a +white suffix adds an estimated white part

    Two details keep the noise model safe on real data. Its power spectrum is kept above 0.5 % (+white) or 1 % (estimate) of the mean (a white floor); an exact Hanning model has zero power at the Nyquist frequency, real spectra do not, and whitening with it amplified that component by orders of magnitude (χ²_red up to 10⁴ on ALFALFA spectra). And the estimate is positive definite by construction, so masks and gaps cannot break the Cholesky factorisation. Differences rather than the sample autocorrelation, because the residuals also contain baseline ripple and line residues at low frequencies, which bias the autocorrelation (on ALFALFA spectra ρ₃ down to −0.27) and which a smoothing kernel cannot represent; differences suppress them and weight the high frequencies that the whitening divides by. Non-negative taps, because every physical smoothing has them; a free kernel turns noise in the higher lags into side lobes with deep spectral notches. The price: ρ₂ of a broad kernel is determined to about 0.03 only, which changes the noise of a flux integrated over a line by about 2 %. The fitted correlation is stored as the metrics noise_rho and noise_rho1. estimate costs one extra pre-fit iteration when the RFI pre-stage is off. Validation compares the options on noise smoothed by a kernel the fit does not know: a close guess written into model.response is as good as the true correlation, estimate is slightly overconfident (coverages 2–5 points low), ignoring the correlation is useless (85 % false positives). Use estimate only when the smoothing is unknown.

    Baseline¶

    Chebyshev polynomials of order K = k_min … k_max with a Gaussian prior (baseline_scale, or mean/covariance from signal-free neighbour spectra via hiprofile.likelihood.baseline.baseline_prior_from_spectra). K is compared by evidence with a flat prior.

    RFI¶

    1. Pre-stage (rfi.prefit): EM fit of profile + baseline with the outlier mixture of Hogg, Bovy & Lang (2010), \(L_j = (1-P_b)\mathcal N(y_j\mid m_j,\sigma_j) + P_b B(y_j)\), with a broad background \(B\), symmetric by default (rfi.background; positive restricts it to excess flux). Output: outlier probabilities \(q_j\) (stored and plotted).
    2. Main fit: channels with \(q_j\) above rfi.threshold are masked (action: mask) or their \(\sigma\) inflated by \(1/\sqrt{1-q_j}\) (action: inflate). A single channel between two outliers is masked with them. Many outliers set the rfi flag.
    3. Extension: model.n_rfi adds narrow Gaussian components (width priors.rfi_sigma_channels, 0.3–1 channel by default) to every model.

    Two guards keep the pre-stage from eating signal. Its line model is not allowed to be narrower than rfi.min_sigma_channels (2) channels, so a bright spike cannot be absorbed as a "line" and hide its own centre from the mask. And a run of outlier channels longer than rfi.max_width (20 km/s, gaps of up to two channels included, at least one channel) is not RFI but signal the one-component pre-stage cannot explain, typically a second line or a narrow dwarf, and stays in the fit; broader RFI needs a larger value or model.n_rfi. The limit protects only runs above the pre-stage model: a run below it cannot be emission and is masked whatever its length (rfi.mask_negative_runs). On ALFALFA spectra these are the dips next to strong continuum sources, which the main fit otherwise reproduced as a positive profile on a strongly negative baseline (see the ALFALFA page). The limit is in velocity so that 1 km/s and 15 km/s channels behave alike: at 15 km/s only single-channel spikes are RFI, and a line narrower than one channel cannot be told from one. hiprofile validate --stage detection measures the completeness with and without the pre-stage.

    Model comparison¶

    For each spectrum the models N = 0 … n_max are evaluated; the baseline order K = k_min … k_max is marginalised inside the likelihood,

    \[ p(y \mid \theta) = \sum_K P(K)\, p(y \mid \theta, K), \qquad P(K) \text{ flat}. \]

    Because the Chebyshev columns are nested, \(A_K\) is the leading principal block of \(A_{K_\max}\): one Cholesky factorisation gives \(\ln|A_K|\) and \(b_K^\top A_K^{-1} b_K\) for every K as partial sums, so marginalising over K costs about as much as a single order. Consequences:

    • one sampler run per N (no K scan, no reweighting, no re-runs);
    • the posterior is exactly marginalised over K; every sample carries \(P(K \mid \theta, y)\), and the order stored with a sample (k_samples) is drawn from it;
    • the per-(N, K) evidences follow from the run without extra sampling, \(Z_{N,K} = Z_N\, \mathbb E_\mathrm{post}[P(K\mid\theta,y)] / P(K)\);
    • an informative baseline prior from neighbour spectra (mean or covariance) fixes K = k_max, because the leading-block trick needs independent zero-mean coefficient priors.

    N = 0 has no nonlinear parameters and its evidence is analytic for every K. For N ≥ 1 a quick fit (MAP + Laplace + importance sampling) runs first; if its evidence lies more than sampler.gate_margin (5) nats below a model with fewer components, the sampler is skipped and the quick evidence kept (method = quick-gated). Otherwise the sampler runs once on the K-marginal likelihood (method = marginal).

    A second, fainter line is a needle in the prior volume: random starts and prior-drawn particles rarely place a component on it. Two warm starts take care of that without touching the evidence. For N ≥ 2 the quick stage also optimises the N − 1 solution with a new component on each of the strongest features of the N − 1 residual (matched filters of 2 and 6 channels, sampler.n_seeds). The SMC then draws sampler.init_fraction (25 %) of its particles from the quick stage's importance proposal and the rest from the prior; the initial importance weights prior/q₀ of this defensive mixture keep ln Z exact and are bounded by 1/(1 − 0.25). Every second MCMC move of the SMC is an independence proposal from the same mixture (exact Metropolis–Hastings), so particles can jump into the narrow mode of a faint second line at every temperature instead of having to random-walk there. On pairs whose second line has S/N ≈ 6 (ln Z of N = 2 within a nat of N = 1) this brought the SMC evidence from 2–5 nats below nautilus to within a nat.

    Model probabilities use a Poisson prior on N (mean poisson_rate) and the flat prior on K; the detection probability is \(P(N\ge 1\mid y)\). Several components are compared through evidences, not trans-dimensional sampling.

    Samplers¶

    All backends implement run(loglike, prior_transform, ndim) -> SamplerResult (samples, weights, ln Z ± error, runtime, number of likelihood calls, statistics).

    backend role
    smc default: adaptive tempered SMC (1000 particles, random-walk moves), one batched likelihood call per step; with --gpu (JAX likelihood) the batch runs as one XLA call
    nautilus most precise ln Z, fewest likelihood calls, but ~10x slower here because its neural-network training dominates for a cheap likelihood; default check_backend
    dynesty second opinion, well-documented diagnostics
    ultranest robustness check, conservative ln Z errors
    quick MAP + Laplace + adaptive importance sampling; quick look and start values, may miss modes

    Modes: --mode quick (quick backend everywhere), --mode full (sampling of all N and K), --mode check (additionally runs check_backend at K* and flags \(|\Delta\ln Z| > 3\sigma\)).

    Every nested sampler has a time / call budget (nautilus 600 s or 10⁶ calls, dynesty and UltraNest 10⁶ calls); a fit that hits it gets the sampler_budget flag.

    Accuracy and speed¶

    Measured on a 512-channel spectrum (S/N 15, first-order baseline), eight seeds per setting:

    backend N = 1: ln Z bias / scatter / time N = 2: ln Z bias / scatter / time
    nautilus (reference) – / 0.02 / 10 s – / 0.03 / 40–46 s
    smc, 1000 particles, 10 moves −0.04 / 0.10 / 0.9 s +0.25 / 0.23 / 3.2 s
    smc, 1000 particles, 20 moves 0.00 / 0.11 / 1.8 s +0.16 / 0.11 / 6.6 s
    quick −0.03 / 0.02 / 0.3 s −0.1 / 0.1 / 0.7 s (low ESS)

    dynesty and UltraNest lie 0.05–0.15 above nautilus for N = 2, so part of the SMC offset is nautilus' own.

    The SMC runs as two independent runs of 500 particles whose samples are pooled and whose evidences are averaged; the spread between the runs gives the evidence error (the incremental-weight variance of one run misses the correlation that the MCMC moves leave between particles). Measured on a single-line spectrum at S/N 8 with K = 0…3 marginalised, six seeds: the K-marginal ln Z_N of N = 1 scatters by 0.15 (reported 0.10), that of N = 2, whose second component has a broad flat posterior, by 1.0 (reported 0.3; nautilus lies within 0.3 of the SMC values). The per-(N, K) evidences of an improbable K carry an extra Monte-Carlo noise of about 1 nat from \(\mathbb E_\mathrm{post}[P(K\mid\theta,y)]\); model probabilities use the sum over K and are unaffected. Offsets of a few tenths in ln Z change model probabilities by tens of per cent at most; use --backend nautilus or --mode check where precise evidences matter.

    Compiled kernel and likelihood¶

    With numba installed (a default dependency), the ring/disk kernel and the Woodbury algebra run as compiled loops (hiprofile.model.fast, hiprofile.likelihood.fast): table-based Bessel functions (Estrin scheme), recurrences for the phase factor and the Gaussian, direct scattering into the folded spectrum, banded whitening and a small Cholesky solve per parameter vector. They agree with the NumPy reference to 1e-12 (tests/test_fast.py). One evaluation of the marginal likelihood (one component, 512 channels, K = 2) takes about 19 µs in batches and 36 µs as a single call; the NumPy path takes about 100 µs and 300 µs.

    Complete fits with the default configuration (N ≤ 2, K = 0…3, RFI pre-stage), one process:

    spectrum before (NumPy kernel, nautilus) now (numba, smc)
    pure noise 15–55 s 3.4 s
    one component 100–220 s 4.9 s
    two components 100–250 s 9 s

    benchmarks/bench_kernel.py and benchmarks/bench_fit.py reproduce the numbers.
