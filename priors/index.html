    Priors¶

    The default priors are calibrated on the fits of the 34 THINGS galaxies in Table 1 of Stewart, Blyth & de Blok (2014) and on the priors the authors used for their low-S/N fits (their Appendix B.2). They are slightly wider than THINGS because a survey contains more dwarfs, more disturbed systems and lower S/N.

    parameter default THINGS fits other references
    Δv gamma, shape 2.3, scale 83 km/s (median 166, 16–84 % 77–305 km/s), ≤ 800 km/s; fitted to the W50 of 25 434 ALFALFA detections 0.1–566 km/s, median 153 (KS p = 0.75 against the gamma prior); two dwarfs with Δv ≈ 0 Stewart B.2: log-normal 2.2 ± 0.3, which the log-normal fit to ALFALFA reproduces (2.19 ± 0.31)
    Δv_rand log-normal, log₁₀ = 1.1 ± 0.2 (≈ 12.6 km/s), 4–60 km/s 8–32 km/s, median 12.4, log₁₀ 1.13 ± 0.16 Stewart B.2: 1.17 ± 0.14; resolved HI dispersions ≈ 10 ± 2 km/s (Tamburro et al. 2009); narrow / broad super-profile components 6.5 ± 1.5 / 16.8 ± 4.3 km/s (Ianjamasimanana et al. 2012)
    α normal(0, 0.25) truncated to [−1, 1] median 0.00, robust σ 0.14; 3 of 34 with |α| > 0.6 (NGC 3077, NGC 1569, Ho I) Stewart B.2: normal(0, 0.2)
    f Beta(1, 3), density 3(1 − f)², median 0.21, P(f > 0.7) = 2.7 % median 0.15, mean 0.23, 1 of 34 above 0.7 (0.93); a uniform prior is rejected (KS p < 0.001), Beta(1, 3) is not (p = 0.13) Stewart B.2: exponentially decreasing with characteristic constant 0.3 (printed as 0.3 e^(−0.3 f), which would be nearly flat; the text describes a decreasing prior)
    v_ctr uniform over the positions where the whole component lies inside the band minus 5 % margins (contain); normal(v_cat, 20–50 km/s) for targeted spectra – Stewart B.2: normal, σ = 20 km/s
    S half-normal; scale amplitude_scale: auto = max(10 × noise of the band-integrated flux, 2 × positive band flux), i.e. noise-set near the detection threshold and flat over a bright source (a narrower prior would split bright sources into pairs); fix per survey – Stewart: flat (improper, unusable for evidences)
    K flat over k_min … k_max, coefficients N(0, baseline_scale²), auto = 10 × rms the misspecification stage checks the K choice with a prior 50 × too wide Stewart: order by maximum evidence

    All priors are simple one-parameter-family shapes (gamma, log-normal, truncated normal, beta, uniform, half-normal). The flux S keeps a half-normal prior so that it can be marginalised analytically; positivity enters the sampling target exactly through the orthant probability (see Inference). f is a sampled parameter with its own prior: an implicit f prior from two amplitude scales would depend on S.

    Components inside the band. With priors.contain (default) every profile component lies entirely inside the band minus the v_margin margins: v_ctr ± (Δv/2 + 3 Δv_rand), which reaches about 1 % of the horn height. The prior is built in sequence: Δv_rand and then Δv keep their shapes and are only truncated further where no line of that width fits the band, and v_ctr is uniform over the positions where the whole component fits. A fixed window therefore does not favour narrow lines, and where the band minus its margins is wider than 1160 km/s the width priors are unchanged. Without it a component could sit half outside the window and take up a baseline slope or the wing of Galactic HI. Several components are labelled in the order of their relative position in that allowed range (not strictly of v_ctr). A posterior pressed against the limit (edge_band_<i>) raises prior_edge: the line is probably cut by the window. Containment needs a uniform v_ctr prior; an explicit range v_ctr: {low, high} stays the range of the centre.

    Rotational width. W50 mixes the rotational width Δv with the random motions, the asymmetry and the solid-body fraction, so Δv cannot be read off a W50 histogram. benchmarks/fit_width_prior.py forward-models it instead: it draws profiles with Δv from a candidate prior and Δv_rand, α, f from the defaults, computes their W50 with the model and fits the candidate's parameters to the quantiles of the observed log W50. On the 25 434 HIcode 1 detections of ALFALFA α.100 (rest-frame W50, median 170 km/s, 0.29 dex, skewed towards narrow lines):

    Δv prior quantile misfit KS distance
    previous default, log-normal 2.2 ± 0.4 dex 0.079 dex 0.106
    log-normal fitted, 2.19 ± 0.31 dex 0.058 dex 0.077
    log-normal × sin i (isotropic inclinations), 2.32 ± 0.27 dex 0.045 dex 0.060
    gamma fitted, k = 2.3, θ = 83 km/s 0.033 dex 0.043
    gamma × sin i, k = 3.3, θ = 74 km/s 0.031 dex 0.038

    The gamma is skewed towards small widths in log Δv like the data and falls off exponentially above 300 km/s like the velocity function of massive galaxies; projecting it with random inclinations hardly improves the fit and is left out. The previous 0.4 dex were a setting without data and predicted far too many very wide lines. The calibration describes the detected population of a flux-limited single-dish survey out to cz ≈ 18 000 km/s; deeper or interferometric surveys (WALLABY, FAST) select differently and should run the script on their own W50 catalogue (--catalog FILE --w50-column ...). The gamma density vanishes like Δv^1.3 towards Δv = 0: face-on or dispersion-dominated dwarfs get their width through Δv_rand, which leaves W50 right but makes the split between Δv and Δv_rand a prior statement (prior_dominated).

    Solid-body fraction. f is the fraction of the HI inside the radius where the rotation curve turns over. In spirals that radius is small compared with the HI disk, so most of the gas sits on the flat part and f is small; only dwarfs whose rotation curve rises across the whole disk approach f ≈ 1. A uniform prior gave such solid-body profiles 30 % of the prior mass, and at low S/N, where the data barely constrain f, it pulled posterior medians to 0.5–0.8 even for 350 km/s wide lines. Beta(1, 3) follows the THINGS distribution and keeps f > 0.7 possible (2.7 %) but rare. Physics also says that f should fall with galaxy mass, but the THINGS fits show only a weak trend with Δv (Spearman r = −0.22, p = 0.22), and Δv is V_rot sin i, so a prior on f conditional on Δv would also penalise face-on spirals; f keeps a single prior.

    The simulator draws from the same priors (hiprofile simulate, all validation stages), so simulated galaxies look like the ones the fit expects.

    Survey-specific settings¶

    • Single-dish surveys (HIPASS, ALFALFA, EBHIS, FAST): standing waves need higher baseline orders; Stewart et al. needed K = 6 for EBHIS. Use model.k_max: 6 and a baseline prior from signal-free neighbour spectra (hiprofile.likelihood.baseline.baseline_prior_from_spectra).
    • Interferometric surveys (WALLABY, THINGS-like cubes): K ≤ 1 is usually enough.
    • Targeted spectra with an optical redshift: priors.v_ctr: {kind: normal, mean: <v_cat>, std: 30}.
    • Evidence comparisons across a survey: set priors.amplitude_scale to a fixed value (e.g. the 95th percentile of the expected integrated fluxes).

    At high S/N the data dominate and the priors hardly matter; at low S/N they regularise the degeneracy between Δv and Δv_rand. Objects outside the THINGS range (very disturbed or very narrow profiles) are pulled towards typical values; the prior_edge flag marks posteriors that pile up at a prior boundary, and prior_dominated marks fits whose Δv posterior is not much narrower than the prior (the width is then a prior statement, not a measurement). The bias study in Validation measures the pull for fixed true profiles: from S/N 8 upwards the posterior median of W50 is unbiased to within 11 km/s; at S/N 5 a 430 km/s line is pulled down by 22 km/s with these priors and by 137 km/s with flat (log-uniform) priors, so the THINGS priors are kept as defaults.

    References: Stewart, Blyth & de Blok (2014), A&A 567, A61 (arXiv:1405.1838); Ianjamasimanana et al. (2012), arXiv:1207.5041; Oman (2022), arXiv:2108.08856; Tamburro et al. (2009), AJ.
